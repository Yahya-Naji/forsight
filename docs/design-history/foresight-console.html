FORESIGHT

An instrument, not a dashboard.

Design system for the Tawazun neurosymbolic foresight console. Every motion pattern here argues the architecture: that each claim is typed, sourced, rule-scored, and refusable.

Direction · operational dark Stack · Next.js + Tailwind v4 + Motion Density · 12–15px, 4px grid Screens · 6 routes
01

Motion vocabulary

Six patterns. Each one makes a rule of the system visible — none of them decorate. If an animation can be removed without losing an argument, it should be. All demos below are live; click them.

Pattern 01 · Chain traversal

Link-independent UAS reduce the coverage of RF-based defeat layers EV-001

EW-02 question EV-001 evidence SIG-EW-04 signal F-EW-01 finding R-EW-04 risk

Why it earns its place. A fade-in would say “here is related data.” A staggered walk says “this conclusion was derived, in this order, from that question.” The stagger is the traceability claim.

Pattern 02 · Gate stamp

The UAE lacks adequate counter-UAS coverage across critical national infrastructure.

Customer validation required

VG-EW-01 OPEN · raised by uae_inference_guard · blocks any UAE-specific capability-gap claim

Why it earns its place. The refusal is the product. A quiet toast would bury it; the stamp lands like a classification mark and makes the system’s restraint the most visible thing on screen.

Pattern 03 · Corroboration merge
EV-001
UK MOD · Tier 1
Class C · 1 source
EV-014
Reuters · Tier 4
Class C · 1 source

Why it earns its place. Two claims physically becoming one is the Class-B rule made literal — “≥2 independent documents.” It’s the single hardest idea to explain in words and the easiest to show in 400ms.

Pattern 04 · Tier ladderhover a source

Why it earns its place. The palette already encodes authority — the accent is Tier 1. Illuminating a rung shows the reader where a claim’s weight came from without a legend.

Pattern 05 · Signal strength
WEAK1 evidence row · no Tier-1 source

Why it earns its place. Strength is computed, never authored. Segments springing up under a “re-run rules” action shows the value changing because the evidence changed — not because someone typed it.

Why it earns its place. This is the live-demo moment: drop in one Tier-1 document and watch a signal upgrade, a gate close and the report regenerate. The rail is what makes that legible from the back of a room.

Tokens

The ordinal ramp is the signature: the accent colour is Tier 1, and authority fades toward slate as tier descends. Semantic colours never borrow the accent — amber belongs to gates alone.

Geometry & motion constants
radius3px controls · 5px panels. Nothing is pill-shaped; soft corners read as consumer software.
spacing4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 36 · 56 — a 4px grid, no arbitrary values.
duration150–250ms for state, 300–420ms for layout. Nothing over 500ms.
easingcubic-bezier(.2,.9,.3,1) default; (.16,1.4,.4,1) for the stamp only.
reducedprefers-reduced-motion collapses every duration to 0.01ms. State still changes; only the tween goes.

06

Build order

Sequenced so there is something demo-able after every step, and so the highest-risk screen isn’t the last one attempted.

01

Token layer & shell

Tailwind v4 with these tokens as CSS variables, the Archivo/Plex pairing, Panel + PillarRail + the app shell. Port the four existing pages onto it.

02

Registers

Evidence and signal tables, DataTable with tier stripes, SignalMeter. Cheap, and it makes the 22 live documents visible immediately.

03

Provenance drawer

Citation → drawer → TierLadder → the rule name. The core argument, and a prerequisite for the report screen.

04

Report & generation trace

Rendered markdown with live citation triggers, plus the trace view. Requires fixing fetch_inputs to honour section links first — the trace would otherwise show “10 withheld: none.”

05

Gates & verification scorecard

GateCard with Pattern 02, then the head-to-head numbers once evaluate.py exists.

06

Ontology graph

Highest risk, highest payoff — built last, on top of components already proven by the screens above.
