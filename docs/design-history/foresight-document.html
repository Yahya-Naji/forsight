Section 1

What is changing

The regionally-active threat landscape is concentrated rather than diffuse. Of 176 active intrusion sets catalogued in MITRE ATT&CK, 36 reference Middle East, Gulf or UAE targeting in their threat descriptions [EV-ATTACK-001]. That is roughly one in five of all catalogued sets, against a region holding a far smaller share of global network estate.

One of those 36 names the United Arab Emirates explicitly. Stealth Falcon (G0038) is described as operating against Emirati targets; a further set, MuddyWater (G0069), carries both Middle East and UAE targeting references. The remainder cluster around Iran, Israel and broader Gulf activity.

Connected defence architectures inherit the vulnerability surface of their control and navigation components. A keyword sweep of the National Vulnerability Database across command-and-control, UAV, GNSS and SCADA terms returns 20 distinct CVE records published in the last 120 days [EV-NVD-001]. The most severe carries a CVSS base score of 9.8; three exceed 7.0.

This is a measure of published exposure, not of UAE exposure. Whether any of these components appear in Emirati defence systems is not determinable from public sources and is not asserted here.

Section 2

What it means for the UAE

The UAE lacks adequate detection coverage against the regionally-active intrusion sets identified above, and its connected defence architecture carries material unpatched exposure in control-system components, creating a readiness gap that should be addressed in the next procurement cycle.

Method and limits

Collection ran across three lanes over a registry of 27 tiered sources: scheduled feeds and scrapes, question-driven search, and structured APIs loaded directly as typed entities without passing through a language model. Twenty-two documents were retained — nine Tier 1, nine Tier 3, four Tier 4.

Two Tier-1 sources could not be collected directly and are reached through site-scoped search instead: NATO NCIA returns 403 site-wide, and the UAE Cyber Security Council fails TLS negotiation. Janes and Shephard Media remain paywalled and are excluded pending a subscription decision. These absences are recorded rather than silently tolerated.

The synthesis stage has not yet run, so this document contains no signals, findings or risks — only evidence. That is why it is a baseline rather than an assessment.
